Engineering Methodology

Hardware Development Lifecycle

Every electronic product moves through a disciplined engineering lifecycle — from mathematical requirement formulation to physical benchtop validation.

01 / STAGE
Requirements

Establish input voltage ranges, current capacities, power dissipation budgets, and component deratings.

02 / STAGE
Circuit Design

Select analog/digital topology, calculate passive resistor/capacitor networks, biasing, and protection snubbers.

03 / STAGE
Simulation

Verify in LTspice: transient response, switching waveform peaks, harmonic ripple, and thermal operating margins.

04 / STAGE
Schematic

KiCad hierarchical capture, component symbol creation, pin direction labeling, and Electrical Rules Checking (ERC).

05 / STAGE
PCB Layout

Physical footprint placement, critical loop routing, decoupling proximity, continuous ground pours, and DRC.

06 / STAGE
Prototyping

Breadboard validation, SMD/THT soldering, assembly inspection under magnification, and cold-continuity verification.

07 / STAGE
Bench Testing

First-time power-on under current-limited supply, DMM rail measurements, oscilloscope PWM/ripple verification.

08 / STAGE
Validation

System functional bring-up, boundary condition testing, thermal observation, documentation, and design reviews.

01 PCB Design • Power Electronics

12V AC → DC Converter (Transformerless Power Supply)

A compact offline AC-to-DC power supply board utilizing capacitive reactance dropping, a 1N4007 full-wave diode bridge rectifier, bulk electrolytic filtering, Zener clamping, and LM7805 regulation for low-power DC electronics.

KiCad 10 AC Capacitive Dropping 1N4007 Full Bridge 1000 µF Filter Cap LM7805 TO-220 Screw Terminals Prototype Design / PCB Layout
01 • Objective
Design Objective & Requirements
Engineer a compact, low-cost power converter capable of powering microcontroller or relay circuitry directly from an AC mains voltage without a bulky, heavy step-down transformer.
  • Input: AC mains through screw terminal block J1.
  • Dropping impedance: Capacitive reactance (zero thermal I²R heating).
  • Rectification: Full-wave 1N4007 bridge.
  • Output: Regulated DC through screw terminal block J2.
02 • Architecture
Circuit Architecture
AC Line → C4 Dropping Capacitor (with parallel R1 bleeder) → Inrush Limiting Resistors (R2/R3) → 1N4007 Diode Bridge → C2 (1000 µF) Smoothing → Zener Clamping → LM7805 TO-220 → C1 Ceramic Decoupling → Power LED (D7) → DC Output.
03 • Schematic Analysis
Schematic Theory of Operation
The AC dropping capacitor C4 (2.2 µF X2 film) introduces reactive impedance: Xc = 1 / (2πfC) ≈ 1.45 kΩ @ 50 Hz. Unlike resistive dropping which dissipates significant heat (P = I²R), capacitive reactance drops the AC voltage without active power dissipation. R1 (1 MΩ) safely bleeds off stored charge upon disconnection.

05 • PCB Layout
PCB Layout & Clearance Constraints
The board is partitioned into two clear zones separated by an isolation boundary:
  • High Voltage AC Zone: Maintained > 3.0 mm creepage distance between AC live and neutral traces. Wide traces for mains current spikes.
  • Low Voltage DC Zone: Ground copper pour with decoupling capacitors placed immediately adjacent to the LM7805 input and output pins.
06 • Simulation
LTspice Transient Verification
Verified the capacitive reactance in LTspice under 230V RMS @ 50 Hz:
  • Confirmed peak inrush current through R2/R3 remains within the 1N4007 non-repetitive surge rating (30A for 8.3ms).
  • Validated voltage ripple across C2 filter cap under simulated 50 mA load.
07 • Challenges & Technical Boundary
Engineering Challenges & Safety Realities
Primary Challenge: Managing peak turn-on inrush current occurring if the circuit is connected at the peak of the AC sine wave (V_peak ≈ 325V). Added series damping resistors (R2/R3) to safeguard the 1N4007 diode junctions.
CRITICAL SAFETY & ENGINEERING BOUNDARY (NON-ISOLATED): This is a non-isolated transformerless power supply. The DC ground is directly referenced to the AC line neutral. It must ALWAYS be enclosed in a non-conductive, touch-proof insulated enclosure and cannot be connected to grounded test equipment (e.g., earth-referenced oscilloscope probes) without an isolation transformer.
08 • Project Status & Future Work
Status: Prototype Design • Next Revisions
Status: Completed KiCad schematic capture, BOM selection, and preliminary 2-layer PCB layout. Verified in LTspice simulation. Fabricated hardware has not been manufactured yet.
Future Revisions: Add an onboard fuse holder and Metal Oxide Varistor (MOV) at J1 input for surge clamping; explore isolated flyback topology for human-accessible applications.
02 PCB Design • Motor Control & Power Electronics

Motor Speed Controller (IC 555 PWM Controller)

A 2-layer printed circuit board engineered in KiCad to modulate DC motor rotational speed via Pulse Width Modulation (PWM) from 1% to 99% duty cycle using an NE555 timer, steering diodes, and an IRLZ44N logic-level power MOSFET.

KiCad 10 NE555 (SOIC-8) IRLZ44N MOSFET 1N4007 Snubber 1N4148 Steering Diodes 99 Tracks / 13 Vias Completed 2-Layer PCB Layout
01 • Objective
Design Objective & Requirements
Create a dedicated 2-layer PCB speed controller for DC motors using PWM. The design must maintain a constant switching frequency while allowing full potentiometer duty cycle adjustment from 1% to 99% without stalling the motor or overheating the driver.
02 • Architecture
System Architecture
DC Battery Input (TP1) → NE555 Astable Oscillator → Potentiometer (J1) + 1N4148 Steering Diodes → Gate Series/Pull-Down Damping → IRLZ44N N-Channel MOSFET (Q1) → Motor Output (TP2) with Anti-Parallel 1N4007 Flyback Diode.
03 • Schematic Analysis
PWM Steering Diode Circuit Theory
In standard 555 astable circuits, changing duty cycle alters frequency. By inserting two opposite 1N4148 diodes across the potentiometer legs:
  • Charge Path: Current flows through diode D1 and the top half of the pot into timing cap C1.
  • Discharge Path: Cap C1 discharges through diode D2 and the bottom half into Pin 7.
  • The total period T = t_high + t_low remains constant (≈ 1.5 kHz), while duty cycle scales smoothly from 1% to 99%.

05 • PCB Layout & Routing
Layout Strategy: 99 Tracks & 13 Vias
  • Layer 1 (F.Cu): Dedicated to high-speed PWM gate signals, steering diode paths, and timing passives.
  • Layer 2 (B.Cu): Low-inductance continuous ground pour providing minimal impedance return paths for high current motor switching.
  • 13 Vias: Strategic thermal and ground stitching vias to eliminate ground loops between timer ground and power MOSFET source.
06 • Inductive Protection
Flyback Diode Snubbing
DC motors have significant coil inductance (L). When MOSFET Q1 switches off rapidly, the collapsing magnetic field generates a reverse voltage spike V = -L (di/dt) that can exceed the MOSFET's breakdown rating. The 1N4007 diode across TP2 recirculates and dissipates this energy safely.
07 • Engineering Honesty & Boundary
Design Scope & Verified Results
Verified: Complete 2-layer KiCad PCB layout (99 tracks, 13 vias, DRC clean). Schematic verified in LTspice transient simulation.
HONEST TECHNICAL BOUNDARY: Do not invent measured motor RPM, motor efficiency percentages, or continuous high-current thermal test data. The design is engineered as a validated 2-layer PCB layout. Continuous operation above 5A would require an auxiliary aluminum clip-on heatsink on the TO-220 package.
03 PCB Design • Analog • STEM Hardware

555 Timer Touch-Activated Buzzer (StemToy-DIY)

A 2-layer educational printed circuit board designed in KiCad featuring exposed immersion gold touch electrodes that exploit human skin impedance to trigger an NE555 monostable pulse generator and drive an active acoustic buzzer.

KiCad 10 NE555P (DIP-8) Skin Impedance Trigger 100 µF Timing Cap Active Buzzer 43 Tracks / 1 Via Completed 2-Layer PCB Layout
01 • Objective
Design Objective & Requirements
Design an interactive, rugged educational printed circuit board demonstrating monostable timer operation. The board must trigger reliably using human fingertip touch contacts without requiring mechanical switches, providing audible feedback through an onboard buzzer.
02 • Architecture
Touch → Timer → Output Architecture
Touch Contact Electrodes → Pin 2 Trigger Gating → 555 Internal Comparator Flip-Flop → Pin 3 Push-Pull Output → Active Piezo Buzzer • Pin 6/7 Discharging C_100uF via R_1K.
03 • Schematic Analysis
Skin Impedance Trigger Theory
The trigger pin (Pin 2) is pulled high to VCC through 10 kΩ in standby. When a finger bridges the exposed touch electrodes, human skin impedance (typically 100 kΩ to 500 kΩ) forms a voltage divider to ground. This pulls Pin 2 below 1/3 VCC, triggering the internal flip-flop. The output pulse duration is governed by: T = 1.1 × R_1K × C_100uF ≈ 0.11 seconds per trigger touch.

05 • PCB Layout & Routing
Layout Strategy: 43 Tracks & 1 Via
  • Direct point-to-point signal routing (43 tracks) with clean 45-degree trace bends.
  • Generous 15.24mm pitch through-hole resistor footprints for effortless manual soldering and STEM education workshops.
  • Exposed top copper electrodes without solder mask for immediate skin contact.
06 • Noise Suppression
Bypass Strategy (1nF on Pin 5)
High-impedance inputs are inherently susceptible to ambient 50/60 Hz mains hum and RF pickup. Placing a 1 nF disc ceramic capacitor directly on Pin 5 (Control Voltage) stabilizes the internal resistor ladder reference and prevents spurious false-triggering.
07 • Engineering Honesty & Limitations
Design Scope & Verified Status
Status: Completed 2-layer PCB layout in KiCad (43 tracks, 1 via). Clean DRC.
HONEST TECHNICAL BOUNDARY: Skin impedance varies depending on skin moisture, pressure, and ambient humidity. In extremely dry conditions, higher contact pressure is required to overcome contact resistance. Does not use capacitive touch ICs (such as TTP223); operates strictly on galvanic skin conduction.
